1. Introduction

These lessons describe important patterns in Thok Nath (Nuer), especially personal pronouns, helper verbs, present and past constructions, negation, subject suffixes and vowel changes.

Notation: 1, 2 and 3 mean first, second and third person. sg. means singular and pl. means plural.
1 pl. (I) means first-person plural inclusive: the listener is included. 1 pl. (E) means first-person plural exclusive: the listener is excluded.
Asterisk (*): An asterisk after a verb marks a verb form used for an action done many times. For example, li̱i̱ŋ* indicates repeated or multiple hearing/listening.
Parentheses ( ): A form in parentheses shows another form of the same verb. For example, tɛɛ (tëë) represents the same te verb in two forms.
Verb-form pairs: Forms such as kɔk/kɔak, math/ma̱th, and lak/la̱k are the same verbs expressed through the vowel/form patterns shown in the verb tables.

3. Short Forms in Everyday Conversation

te, cu, ci̱, bi̱, be, wä are common helpers and verbs in daily conversation. Rather than writing a helper followed by a subject pronoun, a short form can combine the grammatical information.

Te ɣän kɛ bok → Ta̱a̱ kɛ bok.
I have a book.
Te jɛn cieŋ → Tëë cieŋ.
He/she is at home.

4. Helper Verbs

cu — past narrative

cu is used in past narrative, especially when telling a story about what happened.

cu ji̱n jiɛɛn → curi̱ jiɛɛn
And you went.

ci̱ — present perfect / past constructions

ci̱ kɔn ben → canɛ ben
We have come.
ci̱ nɛy nyuur → ca̱kɔ nyuur
We have sat.

bi̱ — future

bi̱ jɛn wä → bɛ wä
He/she/it will go.

be — come

be yiɛn kakä → bia kakä
You come to/from the garden.

wä — go

wä kɛn ŋar → wäkɛ ŋar
They go to play.

5. Negation

ken — past negative

ken makes a past construction negative.

Ken kɛn ben → kenkɛ ben
They did not come / They haven't come.

A slash / may be placed after ken or its shortened form to mark negation.

Present and future negation

A present or future sentence can be made negative by adding / after the ci̱ helper or its shortened form.

/ci̱kɛ mi̱th
They do not eat.
/cä bi̱ ben
I will not come.
/canɛ jɛ guic
I do not see it.

Negative commands

/cu bëë
Do not come. (singular)
/cuarɛ bëë
Do not come. (plural)
/cuanɛ jɛ guic
Let us not see it.

9. Main Sentence Patterns

Negative present

Negative helper + Object + Verb A

/cä jɛ nɛn

I do not see it.

Present simple

Verb B + Subject + Object

nɛɛn kɛn kɔn

They see us.

Suffixed present

Verb C + Object

nɛ̈ɛ̈nä jɛ

I see it.

Passive

Verb E + -kɛ + Object

nɛ̈nkɛ jɛ

It is seen.

With te

Te-form + Verb F

ta̱a̱ nenä

I am sitting.

Negative command

Negative helper + Verb G

/cä nen

I do not see.

Helper + verb

Helper + Verb I

cä nën

I have seen.

Helper + object + verb

Helper + Object + Verb H

bä jɛ nɛn

I will see it.

Core examples

nɛɛn kɛn kɔn — They see us.
nɛ̈ɛ̈nä jɛ — I see it.
nënä — I see.
nɛ̈nkɛ jɛ — It is seen.
ta̱a̱ nenä — I am sitting.
/cä nen — I do not see.
bä nën — I will see. cä nën — I have seen. dëë nën — I can see. ka̱n nën — I did not see.

Compound verb: päl ... i̱kä — to forgive

In simple present transitive sentences, the object can sit between the verb and its particle.

Verb base + subject suffix + Object + Particle / adverbial extension
Pälɛ ɣä i̱kä. — He/she forgives me.
Pälä ji̱ i̱kä. — I forgive you.
Pälkɛ kɔn i̱kä. — They forgive us.
With an auxiliary such as ci̱, the object shifts after the auxiliary: Cɛ ɣä päl i̱kä. — He/she has forgiven me.

Core principles

  • Subject suffixes identify the doer of an action.
  • Short forms are common in everyday conversation.
  • Object pronouns can occur directly after helpers.
  • Compound verbs can split around an object.
  • Vowel changes participate in verb formation.
  • Tone may distinguish lexical meanings in some verbs.
  • Final k is described in the supplied notes as generally pronounced like g, with exceptions involving doubled or otherwise marked k.
